# top cashback: how to find the highest-paying cashback site for the stores you already shop at

Type "top cashback" into a search box and you may be looking for one of two different things. One is a British cashback platform called TopCashback. The other is a question: which cashback site actually pays the most? Both searches tend to land in the same place, because the honest answer to the second question is that nobody wins everywhere.

Cashback rates are set per merchant, they move week to week, and the platform with the best rate on your store today is often not the one with the best rate there next month. What stays stable is structure — how much of the commission a site passes back, how long your money sits there, and what it charges you to get it out. That's what's worth comparing before you pick a default.

## What actually separates one cashback site from another

Four things survive the rate churn:

**Merchant coverage.** A 12% rate is useless at a store you never shop at. Rakuten's US list runs to roughly 3,500 retailers; ShopBack and TopCashback cover the major chains with different edges; Ibotta and Fetch handle in-store groceries rather than click-throughs. Match the network to your actual repeat purchases, not the headline percentage.

**Payment mechanics.** Rakuten pays quarterly on a calendar, with a $5.01 minimum in the US. ShopBack lets you withdraw confirmed cash whenever you want, with no payout calendar. TopCashback has no minimum for cash payouts. Aggressive rate shoppers care about the first number; anyone who's ever waited eleven weeks for a check cares about the second.

**Refund policy.** Rakuten shares a portion of commission. TopCashback says it passes 100%. ShopBack sits in between. A platform claiming full passthrough has to fund itself another way — advertising, premium tiers, or merchant bonus payments — which is fine, just worth knowing when you read "highest rates."

**Tracking reliability.** All of these systems run on affiliate cookies and last-click attribution. That means you can only be paid by one click-through portal per order. Two portals activated on the same purchase makes one of them lose, not both pay.

## Payout terms decide whether a "top" rate is real

A 15% rate that takes four months to clear and three more to withdraw is worth less than a 9% rate you can pull out of the same month. ShopBack's US terms are specific enough to plan around:

- Orders normally show up as **Pending within 48 hours**, when tracking works.
- Cashback moves to **Confirmed** after the retailer's return window closes — typically 30 to 90 days for retail, and after the trip completes for travel bookings. That wait is the affiliate system's, not a ShopBack invention.
- Withdrawals require **$10 in Confirmed cashback**, cap at **$300 per day**, and go to PayPal or ACH.
- Processing is typically within **10 calendar days**, occasionally taking up to 30 depending on bank checks and holidays.
- Cashback is paid in USD, not points or store credit. It's not locked to one retailer.

If your balance never clears, the rate you were promised doesn't matter. Check the store's terms page before you click through — most stores exclude shipping and taxes from the cashback calculation, which means 5% on a $100 order is $5 only when the order subtotal is $100 before those line items.

## The new-user bonus: what's advertised versus what unlocks it

ShopBack's invite signups in the US have been running with a $15 new-user bonus, and the invite flow is how you get it — the code has to be entered during registration, not applied later. In Singapore the equivalent campaign has been $10.

The bonus never lands as spendable money on day one. It shows as Pending and converts only after conditions are met, typically along these lines:

- You signed up through an invite link or code
- You've spent a minimum amount through ShopBack on qualifying purchases
- You've accumulated a set amount of Confirmed cashback from your own shopping — the bonus itself doesn't count toward it
- All of it happens within 180 days of sign-up

Campaign terms change by market and by month, so read the conditions on the screen where you sign up rather than trusting a screenshot from a forum. And don't sign up purely for the bonus: it's a few dollars, and the value of the platform comes from the 40 or 50 online purchases you'll make anyway this year.

## Why cashback goes missing

Most failed claims come down to habits, not fraud detection. The click-through has to be the last one before checkout, and it has to survive the journey.

- **Start the trip on ShopBack**, then go to the store. Not the reverse.
- **Finish on the same device.** Starting in the app and checking out on the desktop breaks the session.
- **Don't open other tabs, coupon sites or social links after activating.** They can register a new referral click and take the credit.
- **Turn ad blockers off** for the purchase.
- **Use coupons ShopBack surfaces.** An outside code can push the order outside the tracking terms.
- **Watch the payment method.** Some merchants don't pay cashback on certain payment types, and said exclusions are listed on the store's cashback info.
- **Shop the right market.** Buying from another country's version of a store usually gets the cashback rejected.
- **Incognito mode** is worth using when a browser is carrying old cookies.

When something still doesn't track, file a missing cashback claim with the order number, date, order subtotal, device and browser version, and a screenshot of the confirmation. Merchants have to validate against their own records, so detail shortens the process.
